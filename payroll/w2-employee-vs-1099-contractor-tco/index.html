Frequently Asked Questions (2026 Verified Data)

How much more does a W-2 employee cost an employer than a 1099 contractor in 2026?

Before voluntary health insurance or 401(k) match, a W-2 employee adds 7.65% in employer FICA (6.2% Social Security + 1.45% Medicare), $42 in federal FUTA, $160–$1,290 in state SUI, and 0.5%–2.0% in Workers' Compensation—roughly 8.5% to 10.5% above gross wages.

What should a 1099 contractor charge per hour to match an $85,000 W-2 salary?

Because a 1099 contractor pays the full 15.3% self-employment FICA tax plus their own health insurance and PTO (180 billable days vs. 260 paid W-2 days), the break-even 1099 hourly rate for an $85,000 W-2 salary ($40.87/hr nominal) is $58 to $68/hour.

What is the cheapest way to pay 1099 contractors and auto-file 1099-NEC forms?

Both Gusto ($6/contractor/mo) and OnPay ($6/contractor/mo) collect W-9s digitally, process next-day ACH payouts, and e-file federal and state 1099-NEC forms at $0 per form.

What are the IRS penalties for misclassifying a W-2 employee as a 1099 contractor?

Under IRS Section 3509 (.gov), unintentional misclassification triggers assessments of 1.5% of wages for income tax withholding plus 20% of the employee's FICA share and 100% of the employer's 7.65% FICA share, plus state DOL back SUI fines.
